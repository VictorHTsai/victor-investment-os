Research Methodology

Compare drawdowns and oversold conditions to historically similar, point-in-time observations. Evaluate 30/60/90-day forward return distributions, event overlap, data freshness, survivorship bias and downside tails.

Sector-specific financial resilience checks are required before an investment candidate is promoted.

RSI and drawdown are descriptive. Exploratory historical analogs use RSI14 ±10 and 252-close drawdown ±10 percentage points, with 90-session spacing, a minimum of five independent matches, and forward 30/60/90-session returns. Current-universe survivorship, limited history, regime dependence and lack of out-of-sample validation remain material limitations. Phase 3.1 compares same-calendar-date 30/60/90-session returns with QQQ and SMH, excluding missing benchmark observations. Reports matched-sample excess median, outperform frequency, and downside p10. This is not a same-universe unconditional baseline, a causal alpha estimate, or an out-of-sample result. Phase 3.2 adds a separate 50%-training chronological holdout with frozen lower-30th-percentile RSI and drawdown thresholds, 90-session embargo and entry spacing, and a pre-scheduled unconditional baseline. It shows no performance claim if fewer than five qualifying signal or baseline events occur; even passing this threshold is exploratory rather than externally validated. Phase 3.3 requests up to 15 years of daily history when the provider and ticker support it; only research summaries, not price-history archives, are stored. The technical dislocation study uses predeclared RSI14 <= 30 AND 252-close drawdown <= -15%, with 30-session spacing and at least five events. It evaluates 5/10/20/30-session close-to-close returns, maximum adverse excursions and first close reaching +10/+15/+20% before a -10% stop within 30 sessions. Closes are not intraday highs/lows; execution, spreads, transaction costs, overnight gaps and options Greeks are excluded. Events can share market regimes, so spacing does not ensure statistical independence. Cross-stock pooling and market-regime conditioning remain future research steps. The Price × Volume experiment separately compares a frozen RSI/drawdown price rule to a high-relative-volume subset (RVOL >= 1.5 using only the prior 20 daily volumes as the denominator). The last half of each available history is a chronological holdout separated by 30 sessions; volume is known after the signal close, so both strategies enter at the following session close; at least five events are required per arm. This is an observational subset comparison, not a randomized test or validated benefit from volume. Both models remain unpromoted until broader cross-stock and multi-period validation. Phase 3.4A adds a final-two-year chronological holdout with a predeclared oversold rule, 30-session target/stop evaluation, next-close entry and 0.20% assumed round-trip cost. One hundred seeded nearby-calendar random entry resamples (within 63 sessions) estimate a descriptive control distribution, not causality, a matched-price-state baseline, or statistical significance; draws and trade outcomes can remain dependent. Phase 3.4B independently reports 2/3/5/10/15-year pre-holdout histories without automatic selection; it is not yet a multi-fold out-of-sample window competition. Even with five events, all edge decisions remain undetermined until stronger independent validation. No validated opportunity score is published. This research is not investment advice.
